02 About

Three years out of school, spent building.

I am Boateng-Kissi Benjamin (Forson), a self-taught developer who grew up in Kwahu and is currently based in Accra. I finished secondary school in 2023 and took a gap year that turned into three. I have spent it interning, teaching myself the parts of software and hardware that school had not reached, and building things other people actually use. I am aspiring to be a computer engineer, and this is the work I am doing to get there.

Most of what I know came from being inside real teams: IT services at iPro Solutions, operations and data at Global Capacity Hub, the IT Section of an Electricity Company of Ghana regional office, and product work at SYNK, where I am now. In parallel I run Sneaker Vault and build the digital tools behind GRAMIC's Foundation.

I work spec-first. What a project has to do and how its data is shaped get written down before any code, then something reaches a running environment early so problems surface against a system rather than a document. Where I have no genuine usage numbers, the case study says so instead of inventing one.

I am currently interning at SYNK and at iPro Solutions while continuing Sneaker Vault and the GRAMIC platform. I am open to internships, entry-level roles and contract work, in Accra or remote, and I would rather flag the edge of what I know than bluff past it.

05
Prototype

Door-Unlocking Mechanism

An Arduino-controlled entry system for shared spaces, built around a practical question: how do you give people access without handing out a copyable key? Codes come in through a keypad, are checked by a non-blocking state machine in firmware, and lock the input out after repeated wrong attempts. The actuator is driven through a transistor rather than a GPIO pin, because a lock's current draw would damage a microcontroller output.

HardwareState machinesArduinoAccess control
